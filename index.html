side quests

When I'm not making music or plug-ins I'm usually trying to get some old game I loved running again. These two are free and open source, up on my GitHub.

RTKContinued a C++ port of Rule the Kingdom

Rule the Kingdom was a city-building RPG on Android from 2012, made by innoWate and put out by Game Insight. I played it for hours with my mom. It needed the publisher's servers for basically everything, so when those shut down the game just stopped working.

RTKContinued rebuilds it in C++, function by function from the original game library, so it runs on a Mac, Linux or Windows again, fully offline and with no real-money stuff. You can play a new kingdom start to finish now. I'm still playing through it and fixing bugs as I find them, so back up your saves. You need your own copy of the original game files.

C++ · SDL3 · about 96% of the game ported

After Midnight

Midnight Mansion HD is Vern Jensen's puzzle platformer from 2011: Jack Malone sneaking through haunted mansions full of levers, keys, secret passages, Medusas and spiders. The Mac version doesn't run on new Macs anymore, so I rebuilt it from the original release.

With the new options off it plays exactly like the original. Turn them on and you get 2–4 player split screen, co-op, a ghost race mode, LAN and online play with friend codes, 89 community mansions, and dumb stuff like moon gravity and Oops! All Spiders. Runs on Mac, Linux, and somehow a PS3.

Meet Henry Malone. He's a whole new playable character I designed for After Midnight, and he's player 2 by default, with his own sprites, so you can always tell who's who in split screen.

Midnight Mansion was created by Vern Jensen.

How these get made: I use Claude Code for the decompiling. Rebuilding thousands of functions from an old compiled game, one by one, is something no person could realistically do by hand, and that's the part it's good at. Everything around that is me: the custom art (like the extra player sprites and the multiplayer screens in After Midnight), directing every step, hours of playing through and logging bugs, and checking it against the original until it's right. The original art and music belong to the people who made those games.
